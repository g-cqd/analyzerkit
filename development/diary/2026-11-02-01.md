# 2026-11-02 — what Litmus needed from analyzerkit (ADR-0030)

Litmus listed fifteen gaps (AK-1 to AK-15). All are in, additive, with tests on real processes and projects; aemi took
its share first (aemi ADR 0028: the process table, durable files, a native SHA-256), and AemiRegex, AemiJSON and
AemiProtobuf were moved to that aemi in lockstep.

What the probes said, beyond what the tests hold:

- A child that leaves its group keeps its session: `setpgrp` changes the group, `getsid` still answers with the dead
  leader's pid. The cleanup is keyed on that, and on start times, so a reused pid is not a target.
- macOS hides the environment of Apple platform binaries; the token test therefore uses Xcode's Python, which it does not
  hide. `proc_pid_rusage` does read a platform binary: Perl allocating 400 MB was stopped 68 ms in, at a 64 MB limit.
- `swift-frontend -print-static-build-config -swift-version 6` reports the compiler's version (6.4.2) as the language
  mode, which is what `#if swift(>=…)` compares; without the flag it reports 5.10. It has no module list: `canImport` is
  answered from the SDK's frameworks, Swift modules and module maps.
- SwiftIfConfig counts a clause inactive when its condition throws, so with the static configuration every
  `#if canImport(…)` clause was inactive under `--define` (ADR-0029), on iOS too.
- swift-service-lifecycle's `UnixSignals` never restores the dispositions it sets to ignored; `TerminationSignals` does.
- swift-subprocess resets the child's signal dispositions and closes inherited descriptors; a disposition a child sets
  still reaches what it runs, which is why the KILL after the grace stays.

Waiting on swift-subprocess: #226 (per-child usage; sampled meanwhile), #371 (a stopped child read as exited; not
reproduced here, covered by the cleanup), #382 (descriptor leak on the fork fallback, which a session alone avoids).
