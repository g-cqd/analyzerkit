# 2026-10-29 (6) — Roadmap 0003: the control-flow graph

## How the catch patterns parse

Asked of swift-syntax 604 before deciding what "catch-all" means: `catch let error` and `catch var e` are a binding of an
identifier; `catch _` is an *expression* pattern around a discard, and `catch let _` a binding of one; `catch let e as E`
and `catch let .a(x)` are bindings of expression patterns; `catch is E` an `is` pattern; `where` is separate. So a catch
item catches everything when it only binds or discards and has no `where`; the five non-catch-all forms are pinned by a
test that stayed green while the others were red.

## Red, then green

Seven tests from the probes K1–K3. The first run crashed the test process on a force unwrap, which hid every other
result; the new tests use `#require`, so a red test fails and the others still report. Red: twelve issues, each for its
stated reason.

The fix: frames gain a kind (loop, switch, labelled block) and pending fallthroughs; an unlabelled `break` skips labelled
blocks, as Swift does; a labelled `do` or `if` is a frame whose breaks join what follows it, and its label is consumed
before its body is lowered (before, a loop inside a labelled `if` took the label); `fallthrough` leaves through the case's
defers into the next case's body; `for try await` adds a throw edge at the head; break and fallthrough share one
"leave scopes, running defers" helper.
